Minutes of the Market Participants Group meeting – 18 November 2026 英国央行 (Bank of England) · 2026-11-19 15:30

Time: 5pm – 6.30pm | Location: Bank of England

Published on 19 November 2026

Item 1 – Welcome

The Governor began the meeting by thanking attendees for joining and reminding participants of the purpose of these meetings. The Governor made clear that the MPC would be in listening mode, and that the meeting would be conducted in accordance with relevant competition and conduct laws, as per the terms of reference.

Item 2 – International developments

Market participants opened by discussing developments in major advanced economies outside the UK. The resilience of global growth and the strength of corporate sector balance sheets, particularly in the United States, were noted. Energy markets were also discussed, with market participants highlighting the inherent difficulty of forecasting energy prices amid continued geopolitical uncertainty.

The material support to global growth from AI-related capital expenditure was noted. Participants also discussed the increasing role of debt financing and the potential implications of a change in expectations around AI for risky asset markets, investment and consumption.

The discussion also covered potential financial market vulnerabilities arising from elevated hedge-fund leverage, sovereign debt pressures and cyber risks.

Item 3 – Domestic developments

Market participants discussed the outlook for the UK economy and the implications for monetary policy. Participants noted the resilience of UK growth this year despite the energy shock. This resilience was broadly seen to have strengthened the case for higher rates, with the November increase having been widely expected. Growth in global AI-related investment was noted as one factor supporting UK activity, although there was some discussion over the extent to which the resilience in domestic growth would persist. Participants also discussed the implications of AI-related growth for domestic inflationary pressure.

Market participants noted the correlation between energy prices and market rates, and how this relationship had evolved over recent months. In discussing the implications of the energy shock, views differed on the relative importance of its demand and supply-side effects. The distinction between implied pricing in the market curve and modal expectations for policy was also noted by market participants.
