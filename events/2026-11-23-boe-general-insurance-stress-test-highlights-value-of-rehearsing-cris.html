General insurance stress test highlights value of rehearsing crisis response and recovery 英国央行 (Bank of England) · 2026-11-23 18:00

The DyGIST 2026 scenario consisted of a sequence of severe yet plausible insurance events unfolding over a three-week period (May 2026), including some significant natural catastrophes and cyber events, coupled with a financial market stress. The exercise involved 25 major insurers, representing more than 80% of the UK general insurance market.

The severe combined scenario was designed to push most participating insurers beyond their internal risk appetite limits, to identify the actions they would take in response. Those actions included use of wider-group resources, including capital injections, credit facilities, dividend retention, alongside significant reliance on reinsurance. This provided insight into the importance of firms being confident that these actions would be available, timely and deliverable in a real market-wide stress.

Insurers were also asked to treat the exercise as a test of their crisis management frameworks, including how they would assess emerging losses and respond in practice to the unfolding events. Those who did so most comprehensively identified the greatest opportunities to strengthen their arrangements, and have made changes to reflect lessons learned from the exercise. Effective responses mobilised relevant functions early, established clear ownership and escalation, made uncertainty visible through evolving estimates and assumptions, and showed how new information and challenge changed decisions. The exercise highlighted the benefit of testing the practical operation of crisis and recovery plans, rather than focusing only on financial impacts.

Reinsurance played an important role in the sector’s resilience in the scenario, reducing gross losses from the insurance events by around 72%. Reinsurance arrangements were well-diversified at a sector level. However, the exercise also highlighted the importance of firms understanding potential concentrations and dependencies within their own arrangements, particularly where resources may be shared across groups or relied upon by multiple firms during a market-wide stress.

The exercise reinforced the importance of insurers continuing to test the assumptions underpinning their recovery plans. Insurers should understand where material concentrations and dependencies arise, distinguish clearly between legally enforceable arrangements and discretionary support, and assess whether that support would remain available, timely and sufficient under a market-wide stress, when groups and counterparties may face competing demands.

Gareth Truran, Executive Director of Insurance Supervision, said:

“DyGIST was a valuable exercise to test how general insurers would respond under pressure to a combination of severe events over a three-week period. It helped shed light on firms' decision-making, the key assumptions underpinning their planned recovery actions, and the important role played by effective reinsurance arrangements.

"The results demonstrated the financial resilience of the sector in the scenario. But as importantly, it helped insurers identify opportunities to improve how they would respond in a real market-wide stress. This should help reinforce insurers' operational preparedness for future events and continue to support policyholders and the wider economy. We are grateful to everyone who participated in the exercise.”

The PRA will use the findings to inform its ongoing supervisory approach, including its work with insurers on the governance and executability of management actions and the resilience of key dependencies.

- A list of participating firms are included in Annex A of the following statement: Update on the 2026 Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test (DyGIST) | Bank of England – the UK's central bank

- DyGIST was designed primarily to support learning and improve understanding of sector-wide responses. It was not a pass-or-fail exercise.

- The results relate to the specific risks and circumstances tested in May 2026 and should not be interpreted as a comprehensive assessment of the sector’s capital position or its resilience in all circumstances.
