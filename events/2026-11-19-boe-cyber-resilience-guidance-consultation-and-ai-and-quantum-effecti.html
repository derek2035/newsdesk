Cyber resilience guidance consultation and AI and quantum effective practices 英国央行 (Bank of England) · 2026-11-19 16:00

UK financial watchdogs clarify cyber expectations as attacks rise

UK’s financial regulators are proposing a new guidance to help financial services firms prepare for and recover from cyber incidents as threats grow.

The Bank of England’s (the Bank) latest Systemic Risk Survey cited cyber as one of the risks that would have the greatest impact on the UK financial system. October 2026 saw the second-highest monthly number of cyber incidents reported to the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) in 5 years, 52% above the previous 18-month peak recorded in May 2025.

In response, the Bank, FCA and Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) are bringing together existing requirements and lessons from supervisory experience to give firms greater clarity about what is expected of them.

The proposed guidance aligns with recognised cyber frameworks, including the National Cyber Security Centre’s Cyber Assessment Framework. It sets out how firms should manage their cyber risk and resilience without imposing rigid rules, giving firms flexibility in how they meet the expectations in a fast evolving threat environment.

It covers key areas, including how firms govern, identify, protect against, detect, respond to and recover from cyber risks and incidents. Firms need strong cyber controls to help keep them safe, support well-functioning markets and protect consumers.

Nikhil Rathi, Chief Executive of the FCA, said:

“Technology moves fast, and so do cyber threats. The financial sector has a crucial role in tackling those threats, including through products like cyber insurance.

"Our response shouldn’t always be more rules. Firms have asked us for clarity about what good cyber resilience looks like. This guidance provides it, helping strengthen their resilience, protect clients and the services they rely on.”

Katharine Braddick, CEO of the PRA and Deputy Governor at the Bank of England, said:

“Cyber attacks are becoming more frequent, complex and dangerous. Regulators need confidence both that firms have strong cyber controls and that they can detect, contain and recover quickly from incidents. Today’s proposed guidance builds on latest understanding to help firms do that.”

Dr Richard Horne, NCSC Chief Executive Officer, said:

“We know cyber threats are becoming increasingly frequent and sophisticated, making it more important than ever that organisations can withstand and recover from cyber incidents.

“The National Cyber Security Centre welcomes this proposed guidance, which will help firms better understand, assess, and strengthen their cyber resilience, in line with our Cyber Assessment Framework.”

Helping firms prepare for AI and quantum risks

Separately, the regulators are publishing additional ‘effective practices’, building on the cyber guidance to help firms practically prepare for specific cyber risks linked to rapid developments in AI and the potential emergence of quantum computing.

The effective practices include firms tackling vulnerabilities to threat actors using AI, and encourage firms to consider the implications of quantum computing, which could undermine the public-key cryptography that underpins financial transactions.

These materials reflect the regulators’ position and expertise that allows them to act on the newest risks in the sector, guiding firms through an uncertain and rapidly developing area.

- The consultation closes on 26 February 2027.

- Firms, trade associations, technology providers and other interested stakeholders are encouraged to respond. Feedback will inform the final guidance next year.

- The proposed guidance is appliable to firms in scope of the regulators' existing operational resilience requirements.

- The approach regulators have taken also reflects the FCA’s focus on smarter, proportionate regulation, providing greater clarity through guidance where new rules are not necessary. This gives firms flexibility in how they meet existing regulatory expectations as cyber risks continue to evolve.

- The proposed guidance covers key foundational cyber risk and resilience areas including:

- Embedding cyber risk and resilience in governance and risk management, with clear roles and accountabilities, appropriate Board and senior management oversight, defined risk appetite, and regular reporting and assurance.

- Identifying and understanding critical IT infrastructure and cyber risk, maintaining up-to-date inventories and maps of critical assets, systems, data and dependencies, and assessing relevant threats, vulnerabilities and potential impacts.

- Protecting critical IT infrastructure from cyber threats, applying proportionate controls including secure configuration, vulnerability and patch management, identity and access management, data protection, and controls over third parties and supply chains.

- Detecting cyber events and incidents promptly, through effective monitoring and detection capabilities, defined alerting and escalation processes, and regular testing of firms' ability to identify potential attacks.

- Responding to and recovering from cyber incidents effectively, with tested response and recovery plans, clear decision-making and communications arrangements, capabilities to contain attacks and restore services, and processes to identify and address lessons learned.

- The additional material on AI and quantum computing is not open for formal consultation. However, the regulators are keen to understand from industry whether this type of support is useful and what they would like to see in future. See Effective practices: Cyber risk and resilience management relating to artificial intelligence (AI) and Effective practices: Cyber risk and resilience management relating to quantum computing.

- A recent Bank of England and FCA survey, found 88% of respondents expect frontier AI cyber capabilities to have a moderate or high impact on their risk profile over the next 2 years.

- In the same survey, 64% said vulnerability management and patching had seen the greatest increase in management attention due to awareness of frontier AI.
